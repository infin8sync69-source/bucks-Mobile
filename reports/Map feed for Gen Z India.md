# Map the recommendations, never the recommenders

Bucks should add a **"Recommended here" layer to the Home map that pins places, not people**. Each point is a Bucks listing, public venue or India Post locality. It gathers the posts tagged there and ranks them by net recommend/not-recommend votes. Tapping a point opens a small preview card, and the card's main button opens the full post in "For you". The evidence makes this the right shape for three reasons. First, every major backlash against a social map (Snap Map in 2017, Instagram Map in 2025) came from **maps of people**, while the features that grew and made money, such as Snap Places, Xiaohongshu's Nearby map and Google Local Guides, were **maps of places and content**. Second, Indians distrust star ratings (only **3% highly trust Google ratings**), which suits a visible "23 people recommend, 4 don't" signal. Third, list views beat map views on task speed, so the map should work as a lens on the feed, not as a new home screen. The differentiation is not visual flair. Bucks would be the only map where Gen Z's "discover on Reels, check with friends" habit and an older shopper's "is this shop any good?" question get answered by the same thumbs-up count, in Hindi, with voice, on a sub-₹15K phone, and with posts that lead straight to a shop, service or ride. One timing issue matters now: MeitY has proposed pulling the DPDP consent and children's-data duties forward to **13 Nov 2026, four days from this report's date**. Whether that was notified is unconfirmed, so the consent and minor-exclusion guardrails belong in a Phase 0 that ships before any map pin goes public.

## Place maps earned users and revenue; people maps earned the backlash

The strongest evidence for a social map is Snap Map. It grew from about **250M monthly users in 2021 to 400M+ by mid-2025**, roughly half of Snapchat's MAU, and Snap pitched it as "about more than getting directions" ([Localogy](https://www.localogy.com/2025/05/snap-map-reaches-400-million-users/); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/social-marketing/400m-snapchat-monthly-active-users-are-on-snap-map/)). Two jobs drive that use. One is seeing where friends are. The other is finding places: in 2021, **44% of Snapchatters said they use the map to find places**, ranked by social-proof labels such as "Popular with Friends" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/07/28/snapchat-adds-my-places-feature-to-snap-map-connecting-users-to-businesses)). The two jobs had opposite results. The friend-location half drew predator-tracking warnings at launch ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/1.4182380)), forced Snap to stop collecting location data from younger EU teens in 2018 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/04/26/snap-to-change-how-snap-map-operates-in-europe-ahead-of-gdpr)), and in 2026 was linked to offenders finding where minors gather ([STV News](https://news.stv.tv/world/a-safe-haven-for-paedophiles-thousands-of-children-abused-on-snapchat)). The places half became a product: Snap launched paid **Promoted Places** in 2025.

Instagram Map shows how close to danger even a content map can get. It launched on 6 Aug 2025 and mixed opt-in "last active location" with a map of location-tagged Stories and Reels. Within a day users were calling it "incredibly dangerous to anyone who has a restraining order." Adam Mosseri said people were **"confused"**: they saw their own face pinned on the map and assumed everyone else could too ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/07/instagrams-map-feature-spurs-user-backlash-over-privacy-concerns.html)). Two senators and later 37 state attorneys general demanded rollbacks ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/bipartisan-senators-urge-meta-roll-back-instagram-maps-feature-rcna223938)). Meta's fixes for the October 2025 India launch work as a design checklist for Bucks. It added a persistent "you are / are not sharing" indicator, **removed profile photos from location-tagged content**, added a preview of how a tagged post will appear, and added parental notifications ([BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/instagram-expands-map-feature-to-india-adds-new-indicators-for-location-sharing-497034-2025-10-07)). Bucks already never shows live location, which removes the main hazard. But a fresh post pinned at an exact point with the author's face is close to live location, so Bucks' pins must carry places and counts, not faces.

Zenly shows the other side. A pure friend-location map reached about **15M daily users out of ~40M monthly**, a very strong habit in Asian markets culturally close to India. It never made money, and Snap shut it in February 2023 ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2022/fearing-competition-snap-decided-to-shut-down-zenly-rather-than-sell-it/)). Bucks gives up that habit loop by design. The evidence says this costs less than it seems, because the revenue in maps comes from places.

The closest model is Xiaohongshu. Location-tagged "notes" get more than **1.5B check-in likes and comments a year** ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395064056_The_dissemination_logic_of_Xiaohongshu's_check-in_behavior_from_the_perspective_of_drama_theory)). Its "Nearby" tab opens a map of notes about local food and fun ([Dao Insights](https://daoinsights.com/news/xiaohongshu-gets-nearby-tab-inching-further-into-local-life-sector/)), and local-services search grew **37% in 2025** ([TMTPost](https://en.tmtpost.com/post/7746909)). Its weakness is Bucks' opening. Chinese users discover on Xiaohongshu but check on Dianping, because social notes can be paid promotion ([Hero Guide](https://www.hero-guide.com/china-travel-tips/xiaohongshu-rednote-for-travelers)). Xiaohongshu's likes are positive-only. Bucks has a negative vote and its own shop, service and ride listings, so it can do **discovery and verification in one app**.

The Indian demand side supports the same design. Reels drives **81% of product discovery** in a Meta/Ipsos study that covered tier 2, tier 3 and rural locations ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/metas-reels-now-drives-81-product-discovery-across-india-study-finds/)). But **76% of Indian Gen Z say instant feedback from trusted people makes them more confident**, and 63% share products with friends or family before buying ([Manifest Media](https://manifest-media.in/marketing/120826/76-of-gen-z-seek-trusted-voices-for-festive-purchases-gwi-and-snapch.html)). Star ratings are distrusted: **46% of Indians have low or zero trust in Google ratings** ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/article/current-affairs/46-indians-have-low-or-zero-trust-in-google-reviews-ratings-survey-122121301028_1.html)), and 56% think e-commerce ratings skew positive ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/56-of-online-shoppers-experienced-positively-biased-ratings-on-ecomm-sites-report-429579-2024-05-14)). A count of real people saying yes or no, with the no visible, answers that distrust better than a 4.3. The gap is in the evidence base: no Indian survey ranks local-discovery channels (Reels, Maps, WhatsApp, friends) for 16–28 year olds, let alone by tier. The "discover then check with friends" pattern comes from national and global studies, not tier 2/3 measurement.

## The differentiated concept: a "Recommended here" lens, not another Snap Map

The product is one toggle on the existing Home map, labelled with a thumbs-up icon and the word "Recommended" (in the user's language). It is not a sixth tab. Snap, Instagram and Xiaohongshu all keep the map as a secondary surface reached from a feed or tab, never the landing view ([Dao Insights](https://daoinsights.com/news/xiaohongshu-gets-nearby-tab-inching-further-into-local-life-sector/)). An empirical comparison of mobile interfaces for location-based information found **lists fastest on every task and maps slowest** ([NTU study](https://nanyangtechnologicaluniv.demo.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/interfaces-for-accessing-location-based-information-on-mobile-dev)). Bucks' brief already has the right flow: the map is an entry point into "For you". The map's job is to answer "what's good around here?" in one glance, then hand off to the feed.

Five choices separate this from a copy of Snap or Instagram. The first is that **pins are places, snapped to Bucks data**. A post appears on the map only when it is tagged to a Bucks listing or public venue, or it falls back to its India Post locality centroid. It never appears at raw GPS. One pin gathers all posts about that place, so the map shows "Sharma Chai Corner: 31 recommend, 3 don't" rather than 34 overlapping dots. The second is that **the signal is net recommendation, shown in words**: "Recommended by 31 people" next to a thumbs icon, never a percentage alone. This shows the negatives that Indian users suspect platforms hide, and it follows BIS IS 19000's rule against suppressing negative reviews ([CAG](https://www.cag.org.in/blogs/regulating-trust-indias-legal-response-fake-online-reviews)). The third is **two plain modes and plain time windows**. "Most recommended" (lasting quality) and "Trending" (fresh momentum) sit next to time chips for "Today" and "This week", because explicit windows are easier to read than an opaque "hot" score. The fourth is **categories that match tier 2/3 youth life and serve everyone else too**. Aesthetic cafés are spreading to Indore, Jaipur, Lucknow and Surat ([Retail4Growth](https://www.retail4growth.com/news/nothing-before-coffee-revenue-jumps-15x-to-inr-90-crore-in-fy26-8097)), and pickleball and thrifting are visible youth trends ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/pickleball-is-ahmedabads-newest-addiction-and-its-reshaping-the-citys-nightlife/2549841); [The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/lifestyle/pinterest-shows-how-indias-youth-is-turning-thrift-into-trendy-1503474208.html)). The same chips ("Food & chai", "Shops", "Services", "Events today") cover a parent looking for a reliable tailor. Ranking within each category stops photogenic cafés from taking over the map, a known effect of check-in culture ([Chaoyang Trap](https://chaoyang.substack.com/p/wanghong-urbanism)). The fifth is **closed-group sharing as a first-class action**. 76% of Indian Gen Z prefer sharing within closed groups ([Snap Newsroom](https://newsroom.snap.com/india-the-gen-z-playbook)), so every preview card gets a WhatsApp share button. Most people check a recommendation with friends before going, and that habit then brings new users back into Bucks.

Ranking should be simple and readable. Sort "Most recommended" by the **Wilson lower bound** of recommends over total votes. It avoids both classic failures: 2-of-2 beating 100-of-101, and a big, divisive place beating a smaller, better-loved one ([Evan Miller](https://www.evanmiller.org/how-not-to-sort-by-average-rating.html)). "Trending" multiplies that score by a Hacker News-style decay, `1/(hours+2)^G` ([Lemmy ranking docs](https://git.join-lemmy.org/asonix/lemmy/src/branch/master/docs/ranking.md)). Use a steep G for events and a gentle G for shops. A soft distance term handles nearness, with a larger radius in small towns. Wilson gives new posts a score of zero, so add a small prior of a couple of pseudo-votes. Two integrity rules matter more than the formula. A place needs a **minimum number of distinct voters** before it can show as "Trending", the same idea as Strava showing no heat until several people have been active ([Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2023/06/strava-heatmap-loophole-may-reveal-users-home-addresses)). And votes should be **trust-weighted** by account age, phone verification and links to the merchant. The exact thresholds (for example, 3 distinct voters to show a count, 5 to trend) are design defaults to tune, not sourced numbers.

Cold start is a real risk in a tier 3 town, where an empty map looks broken. Google's local feed mixes Local Guides, publishers and merchants rather than relying on user posts alone ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products/maps/discover-more-with-google-maps-community-feed)). Bucks can do the same with assets it already has. **Bucks listings and localities form the base layer**, shown as quiet grey pins, and user recommendations light them up. When a viewport has fewer than about five recommended places, the map shows a "Be the first to recommend a place in [locality]" card instead of empty space.

The engagement mechanics should have a practical use. Foursquare's badges and mayorships drove early check-ins and then faded, partly because badges "couldn't be used for anything" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1151888); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/foursquare-abandons-check-ins-better-compete-yelp-google-190298)). 82% of Indian Gen Z say they consciously watch their screen time ([Snap Newsroom](https://newsroom.snap.com/india-the-gen-z-playbook)), which argues against streaks. Use features that do something. "Save to my list" builds a shareable "Weekend plan". "Been there" adds the place to the user's private map and prompts their own recommend vote. "Happening today" shows events and offers that expire. Notifications fire only on concrete value, such as "your post got 10 recommends" or "an event you saved starts in an hour". Gamified contribution does work in India, where Google's Local Guides programme counts the country among its top markets ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-now-50-million-local-guides-adding-content-google-maps-search-284737)). So Bucks can show monthly "top recommenders in [locality]" recognition without a points economy.

## Design for a ₹12,000 phone and a first-time user

The interaction pattern follows from the evidence and should be the same at every zoom level. At **locality and street zoom**, use native clustering: plain coloured circles carrying a count, a category icon and a short label. Rendering a thumbnail bitmap for every point costs an image decode and a texture each ([MapLibre docs](https://maplibre.org/flutter-maplibre-gl/concepts/annotations-vs-layers/)). Only the **top ~10 places in view get large "hero" pins** with a photo and a "Top" tag. Airbnb found that map pins have no visible rank the way a list does, so rank has to be drawn into the pin itself ([TechScoop](https://techscoop.substack.com/p/how-airbnb-made-map-search-smarter)). Leave **heatmaps out of the MVP**. A heat blob has nothing to tap, and Snap uses heat only for "where is it busy" while icons open the content ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/snapchat-map-layers-memories-explore-snap-map-160648193.html)). Heat also brings the sparse-cell re-identification risk covered in the next section. Instead of reloading as the user pans, show a "Search this area" button, which helps on patchy networks.

Tapping a pin opens a **non-modal preview card**. It shows a photo, the place name, "Recommended by 31 · 0.8 km", a large "See posts" button and a visible ✕. "See posts" deep-links into "For you", filtered to that place and scrolled to the top post. NN/g's bottom-sheet guidance applies directly: the Android Back button must close the sheet, a visible Close is required, sheets should never stack, and the design should never rely on swipe-down alone, which users with limited dexterity miss ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/)). Under the map, a horizontal carousel of the same top places, numbered to match their pins ([Berkeley heuristic evaluation](https://courses.ischool.berkeley.edu/i213/s07/projects/naturalist/trac.inaturalist.org/wiki/UIAssignment6.html)), gives users who can't read maps a list to browse. A persistent "Show as list" toggle covers everyone else.

The accessibility rules are concrete. Make every pin's **tap area at least 48×48 dp** even when the dot is smaller (Material; WCAG 2.2's floor is 24 px) ([CVS Health guide](https://github.com/cvs-health/android-view-accessibility-techniques/blob/main/doc/basics/MinimumTouchTargetSize.md)). When pins overlap, tapping opens a short list instead of demanding precision. Add +/− zoom and "My location" buttons so pinch isn't required. Pair every icon with a text label, and never rely on colour alone. Indian HCI research found text-only interfaces unusable for first-time low-literacy users and graphical interfaces most successful, recommending **voice, local language and flat navigation** ([Medhi et al., ToCHI](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ToCHI2711_Medhi.pdf)). It also found that hand-drawn, literal icons beat photos and abstract symbols ([Medhi et al., CHI 2009](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/medhi_chi2009.pdf)). Removing text is not the answer either, because low-literacy users learn to recognise words ([Knoche & Huang](https://researchgate.net/profile/Hendrik-Knoche/publication/266595198_Text_is_not_the_enemy_How_illiterates'_use_their_mobile_phones/links/5849ac7508ae5038263d88aa/Text-is-not-the-enemy-How-illiterates-use-their-mobile-phones.pdf)). So the category chips should be large illustrated icons (a chai glass, a shop shutter, a scissors) with Hindi or regional words underneath, plus voice search and voice playback of posts. **98% of Indian internet users access content in Indic languages** ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/surge-in-indic-language-adoption-leads-to-55-surge-in-rural-internet-users-53632.htm)). That research dates from 2009 to 2013, before Jio, and no recent Indian study tests map literacy, so these patterns need a field test in a real tier 3 town.

Device constraints matter more than data constraints. Average use is about **31 GB per user per month** ([Deccan Herald/Nokia](https://www.deccanherald.com/technology/indias-average-monthly-mobile-data-usage-per-user-hits-31gb-in-2025-nokia-3950714)). Meanwhile memory-cost inflation is pushing up prices in the sub-₹15K segment ([GSMArena/Counterpoint](https://www.gsmarena.com/counterpoint_1_in_5_smartphones_shipped_in_india_in_2025_were_premium_models-news-71366.php)). No published benchmark exists for marker counts on low-end Android, and none at all for osmdroid. The plan is therefore an engineering assumption to verify. The server does the aggregation: PostGIS groups posts by venue and locality and returns at most a few hundred ranked places per viewport, cached per locality. The client uses osmdroid's marker clustering for the rest. Thumbnails load lazily and only for hero pins. Frame rate should be profiled on a 2–3 GB RAM phone before launch.

The homepage gets more engaging from this without becoming fancy. The Home tab opens on the map with the "Recommended" layer on by default, but at locality zoom and with only the top places shown. A one-line strip above the map reads "12 places recommended near [Locality] this week". These three things give a first-time visitor a reason to tap within seconds, while the search bar and Services stay one tap away for people who came to book something.

## Safety and Indian law decide the defaults

Three ways location leaks out matter even without live location. The first is a precise pin on a post made at home, a hostel, a PG or a school. The second is pattern-of-life, where one person's posts cluster around their home or workplace. The third is "trending" hotspots that show where young people gather. Strava shows that aggregation alone doesn't protect people. Researchers inferred home addresses from its heatmap with **37.5% likelihood** for active users, with better results in sparse areas ([Childs et al., ConPro 2023](https://conpro23.ieee-security.org/papers/childs-conpro23.pdf)). Its 2018 heatmap also outlined military bases ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2018/01/29/strava-user-heatmap-reveals-patterns-of-life-in-western-military-bases/)). Small Indian towns are exactly those sparse areas. Venue and locality snapping is therefore the main privacy control, not an optional extra. It should be backed by stripping EXIF/GPS data on upload, a user-set home privacy radius, and **no author avatar or handle on the map** (Snap strips usernames from map content, [ithinkdiff](https://www.ithinkdiff.com/snap-map-view-global-stories-on-a-map-from-friends-and-places-on-snapchat/)). Residential addresses can never become pins, which also stops "not recommended" from being aimed at a private home.

The legal calendar is close. The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 Nov 2025. Notice, consent, children's-data and data-principal-rights duties apply from **13 May 2027**, and MeitY has proposed moving them to **13 Nov 2026** ([AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/?p=87811); [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/)). No source after March 2026 confirms whether the change was notified, so Bucks should build to the earlier date. A precise geotag tied to an account is almost certainly personal data. Bucks should not rely on the Act's "publicly available" exclusion, because Bucks itself derives and aggregates the map placement ([India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-act-publicly-available-personal-data-46899.html/)). In practice that means a separate, itemised consent ("Show my posts on the map") and a one-tap withdrawal that removes past pins. The **under-18 rule is the hardest constraint**. Section 9(3) bans tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted ads aimed at children, and parental consent cannot make them lawful ([Tsaaro](https://tsaaro.com/blogs/safeguarding-minors-online-understanding-parental-consent-obligations-and-behavioural-monitoring-restrictions-under-the-dpdpa-and-dpdp-rules)). "Trending near you" ranking from a minor's device location plausibly counts as tracking. So minors' posts stay off the map entirely, and minors see a non-personalised, locality-level map. This is also the strongest answer to the 2026 evidence on offenders using Snap Map to find teens ([STV News](https://news.stv.tv/world/a-safe-haven-for-paedophiles-thousands-of-children-abused-on-snapchat)).

The intermediary and consumer rules shape operations. Since **20 Feb 2026 the IT Rules require government and court takedown orders to be actioned within 3 hours**, with tighter deadlines for non-consensual intimate imagery whose exact figures sources disagree on ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/india-introduces-mandatory-labelling-for-ai-and-3hour-takedown-for-illegal-content); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/amp/story/news/law-policy/centre-amends-it-rules-to-regulate-ai-generated-content-takedown-timelines-reduced)). Removing a pin must therefore reach the cached tiles within minutes. A map-specific "this shows my home / me" report should have a 2-hour target. AI-generated media must carry its label onto map cards and should not trend without review. Bucks also needs a **per-district kill switch** for orders or unrest, as Snap had when it disabled its heatmap in Ukraine ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/snapchat-disables-heatmap-feature-ukraine-154342214.html?src=rss)). A recommend count that ranks businesses works as a rating. Under the CCPA Dark Patterns Guidelines, fake reviews and paid promotion that looks organic count as **"disguised advertisement"** ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/ccpas-guidelines-on-dark-patterns-an-overview/1844140)). So any boosted pin is labelled "Sponsored", styled differently, and never counted toward "Most recommended". Only 9% of Indians say platforms make sponsored reviews easy to spot ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/56-of-online-shoppers-experienced-positively-biased-ratings-on-ecomm-sites-report-429579-2024-05-14)), so clear labelling also sets Bucks apart.

| Default | What it prevents | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Snap to venue or locality; never raw GPS; strip EXIF | Home, hostel or school doxxing | Strava ConPro 2023; FBI EXIF case |
| No author face or handle on pins; per-author cap on map appearances | Pattern-of-life and "am I on the map?" confusion | Instagram Map 2025 fixes; Snap |
| Under-18 posts excluded; minors get a non-personalised view | DPDP s.9(3); grooming hotspots | Tsaaro; STV 2026 |
| 30–60 min publish delay for non-venue posts; trending pins expire after 24–48 h | Real-time coordination of harassment | Snap Our Story practice |
| Minimum distinct voters/authors before trending | Sparse-area re-identification; false social proof | Strava; CCPA |
| Separate map consent, one-tap withdrawal, "my posts on the map" page | DPDP notice and consent; trust | AZB; Instagram backlash |
| 3-hour takedown path, district kill switch, Sponsored labels | IT Rules safe harbour; CCPA dark patterns | Hogan Lovells; Mondaq |

Women need specific attention in the design, not just policy. Women make up most shared-device users, and families often monitor their phone use "for their safety" ([LEAD at Krea](https://ifmrlead.org/whose-phone-is-it-anyway-how-women-users-navigate-shared-access-privacy-and-leisure-in-india/)). Researchers recommend hiding content and supporting multi-user devices ([Sambasivan et al., SOUPS 2018](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2018/presentation/sambasivan)). Three affordances help a young woman in a tier 2 town recommend a café without risk: posting without a face, an option to tag a post to the locality rather than the venue, and keeping saved lists and "Been there" history private by default. No India-specific data exists on how women feel about location in social apps. That belongs in the Phase 1 field research.

## A three-phase rollout that ships the safe core first

| Phase | Scope | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Guardrails (2–3 weeks, before any public pin)** | Map consent toggle and itemised notice; EXIF strip; venue/locality snapping using listings and India Post localities in PostGIS; under-18 exclusion; author caps; report button and takedown path to tiles; district kill switch; "my posts on the map" settings page | Legal sign-off against the 13 Nov 2026 reading of DPDP; zero minor posts eligible in test data |
| **1 — MVP "Recommended here" (6–8 weeks, 2–3 pilot tier 2 cities)** | Toggle on the Home map; place pins aggregating posts; Wilson-ranked "Most recommended" with "This week" window; clusters with count, category icon and label; top-10 hero pins; preview card → deep link into "For you"; synced numbered carousel and list toggle; 4 illustrated category chips in Hindi plus one regional language; "Search this area"; cold-start base layer and "Be the first" card; WhatsApp share | Map→For-you open rate; recommend votes per map session; % of new posts tagged to a venue; 60 fps target and no ANRs on 2–3 GB RAM phones; field test with older and low-literacy users |
| **2 — Habit without dark patterns** | "Trending" mode with decay and "Today" chip; "Happening today" events and offers; Save-to-list and "Been there"; "Recommended by people you follow" label; voice search and voice playback; trust-weighted votes and ring detection; labelled Sponsored pins | Return visits to the map tab, lists shared to WhatsApp, report rate and vote-ring flags stable or falling |
| **3 — Local depth** | Optional city-zoom "busy areas" shading only where cells clear a k-anonymity floor; locality "top recommenders" recognition; merchant replies and dispute flow; notifications on saved-place offers; more regional languages; tier 3 expansion | Retention among map users vs non-users; merchant conversion from map-originated sessions |

Phase 1 is deliberately small: one layer, one ranking, one card, one deep link. That small scope tests the core hypothesis behind the whole plan, that a place-pinned recommend count pulls people from Home into "For you" and into Bucks' commerce. No public study shows that maps raise engagement over lists. The informal evidence leans toward lists for decisions ([Mamikos UX case study](https://medium.com/mamikos-design/ux-case-study-list-view-vs-map-view-a9a8fc619aa9)), so Bucks should A/B test the default-on layer against a default-off one rather than assume the map wins.

## Conclusion

The way to make Bucks' map engaging is not to imitate Snap Map's playfulness. It is to answer a question that tier 2/3 users of every age already ask and that no current product answers well: "would people from around here recommend this place, yes or no?" Snap and Instagram show that the risk in social maps comes from showing people. Xiaohongshu shows that the value is in local content, and that it leaks to a second app when trust is missing. Bucks can keep the value and skip both problems by mapping places, showing the "no" votes, and connecting each recommendation to a listing someone can actually book or visit. The safest design and the most differentiated one turn out to be the same design.

Three uncertainties should shape what Bucks measures, not whether it builds. There is no tier-split Indian data on how young people discover places. Whether DPDP's earlier date was notified is unknown. And there are no benchmarks for osmdroid on low-RAM phones. Each one is resolved by the Phase 0 and Phase 1 exit criteria rather than by more desk research. The thing to watch most closely is the share of new posts that users tag to a real venue. If that number grows, the map becomes something only Bucks has: a locally owned, vote-weighted guide to towns that Google Maps covers only thinly.
