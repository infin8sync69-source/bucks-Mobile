# Make providers pay for proof, not promises

Bucks' core plan (flat monthly fees, UPI Autopay through Razorpay, sold outside the apps, paid status decided only by signed webhooks) is the right shape for India in late 2026, and the prices are low by every local yardstick: ride apps now charge drivers ₹25–90 a day, Justdial averages about ₹1,600 a month per paid campaign and IndiaMART about ₹3,500. The risks are elsewhere. Getting the mandate approved and getting each monthly debit through are the hard parts: Razorpay says nearly 30% of subscribers drop off before registration, NPCI caps each debit at one attempt plus three retries outside peak hours, and about 20 million UPI mandates are revoked every month. Providers also stay only while leads keep arriving. IndiaMART's smallest monthly payers churn 5–7% a month. Before launch Bucks should change eight things. It should fix how the founding discount is applied (the Razorpay 50% offer charges ₹99.50 while the database expects ₹99, and late approvers lose the discount entirely), add a notice before the founding price steps up in month 13, and split a customer's "paused in my UPI app" from a bank failure. It should run one grace clock from the due date, add a one-off "pay this month" link for providers who refuse a mandate, move reminders from push-only to WhatsApp first with SMS fallback, trigger the plan ask on the first lead rather than only on day counts, and send a monthly lead report. Before the first founding free period ends, it also needs four things it does not have yet: a GST position, live Razorpay keys, DLT-registered SMS templates, and an iOS plan card with no purchase cues. With those changes, ₹199/₹299/₹499 is a defensible, low-friction price. Without them, the module will lose more money to silent payment failures and confusion than to price objections.

## Bucks is cheap against every anchor, so proof of leads decides renewal

Indian providers already pay platform fees, mostly far higher ones. Ride-hailing has moved drivers from 20–35% commissions to flat access fees. Rapido charges autos ₹5–29 a day ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/start-ups/after-cabs-rapido-extends-saas-based-zero-commission-model-to-auto-drivers-124021300846_1.html)), Uber charges autos ₹20–40 a day ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/19/uber-ditches-commissions-in-favor-of-fees-for-auto-rickshaw-drivers-in-india)), Namma Yatri charges ₹25 a day for autos and ₹90 for cabs ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/article/startups/ola-uber-rapido-how-cab-aggregators-are-responding-to-subscription-vs-commission-model/96379/1)), and Ola sells a 30-day cab pass at **₹2,010, exactly 30 times its ₹67 daily rate** ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/06/223-ola-expands-zero-commission-model-to-cab-drivers/)). In October 2025 Uber moved all Indian drivers to daily and monthly subscriptions ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/uber-subscription-model-for-drivers-gst-impact-ride-hailing-shift-125101001289_1.html)). Against that, Bucks' Taxi plan at ₹299 a month is about ₹10 a day. It is not a replacement for a driver's main app. It is an add-on that brings direct customers on top of an existing pass, and the copy should say so.

Listing businesses show the same headroom. Justdial's average realisation was **₹4,825 per paid campaign per quarter** in Q1 and Q2 FY26, about ₹1,600 a month ([ICICI Direct](https://www.icicidirect.com/research/equity/trending-news/just-dial-reports-2-qoq-and-6-yoy-revenue-growth-in-q2fy26)). IndiaMART's ARPU is about ₹42,000 a year ([IndiaMART Q2 FY26 transcript](https://investor.indiamart.com/files/IndiaMART_Q2_FY2025-26_Transcript_Earning_Call.pdf)). Tradespeople on Urban Company give up 20–30% of each job ([ValueForStartups](https://valueforstartups.in/09-urban-company)), and a 2021 beautician protest forced the commission cap down from 30% to 25% ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/urban-company-cuts-commission-to-25-from-30-after-workers-protest)). ₹199 a month is roughly a tenth to a third of one day's work for an electrician or plumber earning ₹500–2,500 a day ([Solve24](https://solve24.in/compare/plumber-vs-electrician-career)). The closest analogue to Bucks' price point, the Paytm Soundbox, shows that **₹100–150 a month is an accepted micro-merchant fee**. It also shows those merchants switch quickly when something cheaper turns up ([Outlook Business / BofA](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/enterprise/bofa-research-paytm-soundbox-first-to-bring-audio-based-payments-confirmations-dominates-the-market-6795)).

Affordability is therefore not the barrier. Belief that leads will come is. Sulekha's complaint record shows how paid listings fail: a ₹5,000 package produced two leads in a month, and the complaints are about fake leads and refused refunds ([India Consumer Forum](https://www.indiaconsumerforum.org/complaint-regarding-business-leads-package-from-sulekha/)). IndiaMART's churn follows lead flow and commitment. Monthly payers churn about **7% a month**, annual Silver about 4%, and Gold/Platinum about **1%** ([IndiaMART transcript](https://investor.indiamart.com/files/IndiaMART_Q2_FY2025-26_Transcript_Earning_Call.pdf)). Its paying-supplier count recently slipped while ARPU rose ([Whalesbook](https://www.whalesbook.com/corporate-news/English/technology/IndiaMART-Q2-Revenue-Grows-10percent-YoY-Paying-Supplier-Base-Declines-Slightly/6ae59889c6c6dccbb90d89be)). For planning, assume 3–8% of providers convert from a free period to paid (the ChartMogul no-card trial benchmark is 4–6% good and 10–15% great ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/))), and assume 5–7% monthly churn on monthly plans. That loses roughly half of monthly payers within a year unless lead flow or annual plans hold them.

Two pricing conclusions follow. First, keep the flat fee and make it visibly fair. Unions call driver subscriptions "a rebranded commission structure" and attack fees that rise or feel compulsory ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/ola-uber-the-zero-commission-u-turn/)). A published price lock and no per-ride add-ons protect Bucks from that attack. Second, frame each price per day, next to the true monthly figure: Skills under ₹7 a day, Taxi about ₹10, Business about ₹17. Gourville's experiments show that "pennies-a-day" framing makes people compare a price with small everyday spending, and that it works only while the daily figure stays small ([JCR 1998](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v24y1998i4p395-408.html); [Marketing Letters 2003](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1025467002310)). All three plans are in that range. Showing the monthly figure as well is not optional: a LocalCircles panel of 141k+ found **63% of Indian payment users report hidden charges**, and the CCPA has banned 13 dark patterns ([LocalCircles](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/online-payment-dark-patterns)).

Yearly at 10× monthly is sensible, since annual payers churn about half as much as monthly ones. But few drivers have ₹2,990 to spare at once, so keep monthly as the default and offer annual (and possibly quarterly) as an upgrade. The founding offer has two predictable conversion cliffs: month 4, when the free months end, and month 13, when the half price ends. The current draft prepares for the first but not the second.

## The mandate is a trust problem before it is a payments problem

UPI Autopay regulation favours the provider, and Bucks should say so. The RBI's consolidated **E-mandate Framework of 21 April 2026** (RBI/DPSS/2026-27/396) replaces the 2019–24 circulars. It requires authentication at registration and on the first debit, allows later debits up to **₹15,000 without a PIN**, makes the *issuer* send a pre-debit notice at least 24 hours ahead, and lets the customer opt out of a single debit or withdraw the mandate at any time, free of charge ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13374)). Every Bucks price, ₹4,990 yearly included, sits far below the ₹15,000 ceiling. Razorpay sends the pre-debit notice through the bank and by default debits about 25 hours after it is delivered ([Razorpay UPI recurring](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/recurring-payments/upi.md)). Bucks has no legal duty to send its own pre-debit message. It should still send one, because a surprise debit leads to a revocation.

Distrust is real and recent. **68% of respondents** in LocalCircles' April 2026 survey reported a "subscription trap" when linking a bank account for UPI payments ([LocalCircles](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/online-payment-dark-patterns)), and Moneylife warns readers that a "₹2 trial" can quietly become a ₹599 debit ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/fraud-alert-that-2-trial-could-quietly-turn-into-a-599-debit/81632.html)). This matters for Bucks' trial design. Razorpay's free-trial pattern is a subscription with a future `start_at` and no upfront amount, which takes a **₹5 authentication charge that is refunded automatically** ([Razorpay workflow](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/subscriptions/workflow.md)). That is exactly what `billing-link` creates for providers who approve during their free period. To a wary provider it looks like the trap the press describes, unless the link message and the in-app explainer say it first: "₹5 to check your UPI, returned to you. Your UPI app will show up to ₹199. We take ₹99 a month until Dec 2027, then ₹199." The mandate's maximum should equal the full plan price and never be higher.

Registration is the steepest drop. Razorpay itself says **nearly 30% of subscribers drop off before registration** ([Razorpay blog](https://razorpay.com/blog/upi-autopay-with-intelligent-revenue-protect/)). Where people fall off is predictable: the link opens inside WhatsApp's own browser and fails to hand off to a UPI app, the provider picks the wrong app, the "maximum amount" alarms them, they hesitate at the PIN, or they come back unsure whether it worked. Bucks' app can't link to the payment, but it can explain in advance what the provider will see, which Play allows (next section). Adoption among micro-merchants runs through people. In a CFI survey of 50 merchants, every one said owners or family members handle payments, and peers' adoption lowered others' fear ([Center for Financial Inclusion](https://centerforfinancialinclusion.org/the-digital-payments-push-what-50-micro-merchants-in-india-are-saying)). Soundbox reached **1.37 crore subscriptions** largely through a field force ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/11/paytms-q2-ai-soundbox-cautious-postpaid-revival-bnpl-lending)). Bucks' field-rep channel is therefore an asset to build on: reps and family members should be able to approve on the provider's behalf, from their own phone if need be.

The trial structure needs one honest caveat. Globally, trials that demand payment details up front convert two to three times better per trial but attract far fewer sign-ups ([Visionary Marketing summary of First Page Sage](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/saas-free-trial-conversion-statistics-2026)). RevenueCat finds India and Southeast Asia convert worst, with monthly-plan trials peaking at 5–9 days ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/free-trial-length)). Those are consumer apps where value arrives at once. A Bucks provider sees value only when a customer calls, so a 7-day trial would end before the first lead in thin towns. Keeping 30 days with no payment details is right for supply. The improvement is to make the ask follow value rather than the calendar: invite the mandate after the first call or WhatsApp lead ("Ramesh found you on Bucks. Keep getting customers for ₹7 a day."), and offer an optional early mandate that locks the founding price. That keeps the conversion benefit of collecting payment details early without making it a gate.

## Most lost revenue will be failed debits, not cancellations

Execution failure on UPI Autopay is severe. Mint, citing NPCI data, reported **55–90% of AutoPay debits failing** in some banks in August 2025, mostly for insufficient balance ([Mint via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/en/stories/newspaper/Mint-Mumbai/UPI-AUTOPAYS-ENDLESS-WOES-FORCING-AN-INDUSTRY-RETHINK)). Razorpay's own estimate is about 20% of subsequent debits ([Razorpay blog](https://razorpay.com/blog/upi-autopay-vs-card-e-mandates/)), and technical declines are only about 0.5% ([The Mainstream](https://themainstream.co.in/?p=105895)). About **20 million mandates are revoked a month**, mainly after low-balance failures ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/upi-autopay-revocations-hit-20-mn-monthly-over-low-customer-balances-125090700500_1.html)). The rails leave Bucks little control. NPCI's May 2025 circular allows **one attempt plus three retries**, run only outside the 10:00–13:00 and 17:00–21:30 peaks ([Digit](https://www.digit.in/news/general/upi-alert-npcis-new-rules-kick-in-from-august-1-heres-what-changes.html)). Razorpay retries on T+1, T+2 and T+3, then marks the subscription `halted`. After that it never charges the missed invoice again, and a UPI subscriber cannot switch to a new UPI mandate from Razorpay's hosted page ([Razorpay payment retries](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/subscriptions/payment-retries/)). Once halted, recovery depends entirely on messaging and a fresh link, which Bucks' design already uses (`billing-link` creates a new subscription and cancels the old one).

Recovery benchmarks are mostly vendor figures. Retries alone recover roughly 25–35%, and retries plus a well-timed message sequence about 45–57% ([Stripe recovery analytics](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/revenue-recovery/recovery-analytics); [Redux Payments](https://www.reduxpayments.com/blog/stripe-smart-retries-explained)). There is no UPI-specific benchmark. What can be designed is clear. The `subscription.pending` payload carries the payment entity with an error reason ([Razorpay webhooks](https://razorpay.com/docs/webhooks/subscriptions.md)), so each message can match the cause: low balance means "keep ₹199 in the account, we try again tomorrow"; bank down means "nothing to do"; a revoked mandate means "approve again"; after a halt it is "pay once". Pausing should also be a first-class choice, because UPI apps let customers pause mandates natively and vendor data says many people who pause return ([Recurly](https://recurly.com/blog/the-pause-button-your-best-retention-tool)). Treat a failure as "low balance today", not as intent to leave. Treat a revocation as a separate, voluntary signal and measure it separately.

Channels decide whether those messages arrive. Since **1 October 2026 a WhatsApp utility template costs about ₹0.115** in India, and utility messages inside the 24-hour service window are no longer free ([Meta pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing.md); [TelecomTalk](https://telecomtalk.info/whatsappbusiness-price-reveal-telegram-arattai-may-benefit/1012403/)). A transactional SMS costs about ₹0.15–0.25 ([richautomate](https://richautomate.in/blog/sms-api-pricing-india-2026)). Marketing templates cost about ₹0.86, so win-back wording must not leak into utility templates. Meta accounts must move to INR billing by 31 December 2026 or delivery stops on 1 January 2027 ([Meta pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing.md)). Every SMS header, template and URL must be registered on DLT, and SMS containing unregistered links have been blocked since September 2024 ([Tanla](https://tanla.com/blog-posts/trais-whitelisting-directive-explained-everything-you-need-to-know)). Push alone fails: Android 13 notification opt-in averages about 60% ([Netcore](https://netcorecloud.com/?p=94067)), so roughly 40% of providers would never see the current draft's reminders, which are push-only. Razorpay already emails and texts customers on creation, charge, failure, halt and cancellation ([Razorpay notifications](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/subscriptions/notifications.md)). Bucks' messages should add context, such as the listing name, leads this month and one action, rather than repeat Razorpay's receipts.

## The stores, GST and DPDP set hard edges around the module

Selling outside the app is the right call, and it should be stricter on iOS than on Android. Google Play's Payments policy exempts physical goods and services but **does not list advertising or visibility**, so a listing plan counts as a digital service under Play Billing by default ([Google Play Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en)). Google explicitly allows any app to be "consumption-only" and to tell users, without a link, that they can buy elsewhere. Its own examples include "Go to our website to upgrade your subscription to Premium". Talking to users outside the app is allowed ([Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)). Bucks' current in-app line, "We've sent the plan details to your phone by SMS", fits that allowance. A "Send link again" button is a grey area. A pay button, a copyable rzp.io URL or a WhatsApp deep link with the URL is not allowed. Apple is harsher. Guideline **3.1.3(g)** says buying "boosts" displayed in the same app must use in-app purchase, and 3.1.3(b) lets apps unlock web-bought subscriptions only if they are "also available as in-app purchases" ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). An iOS card that shows status only is the usual practice, but it carries rejection risk. It should contain no prices, no "we sent you a link" and no wording about how to pay. If Bucks ever wants payment inside the Android app, India's alternative billing program cuts Google's fee by 4 points (for example 15% to 11%) ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)). At ₹199 that is still about ₹22 a month, versus about ₹5 through Razorpay.

GST is the largest unresolved item in the draft. The ledger already splits 18% GST out of every charge (`amt - round(amt/1.18)`), which assumes Bucks is GST-registered and issues tax invoices. Two questions need a CA. The first is registration and invoicing. If Bucks is not yet registered, it cannot charge "GST included". If it is, each paid cycle needs a compliant tax invoice with SAC, place of supply and the recipient's GSTIN when the provider is registered, and Razorpay's subscription invoices may not meet that unless configured. The second matters more for Taxi. The Karnataka AAR held in November 2024 that Uber still owes **5% GST as an e-commerce operator under Section 9(5)** on rides even under its subscription model. An earlier ruling for Namma Yatri held the opposite, and the question went to the GST Council ([VJM Global](https://www.vjmglobal.com/blog/uber-is-liable-to-pay-gst-as-e-commerce-operator-on-new-subscription-based-model); [Jurishour](https://www.jurishour.in/gst/gst-applicability-uber-subscription-passenger-transport-model/)). If riders book rides through Bucks, that exposure exists whatever the drivers' plan is called. Providers' subscription fees, by contrast, are Bucks' own supply. They stay outside Section 194-O TDS and Section 52 TCS as long as Bucks never collects money for providers' sales ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/section-194o)).

Data protection is manageable if it is built in now. The **DPDP Rules were notified on 13–14 November 2025**. The consent-manager rule takes effect after one year, around now, and most fiduciary duties (notice, security safeguards, breach reporting, retention and erasure) apply from about **13–14 May 2027** ([PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf); [AZB Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/)). The founding cohort will still be paying then. Bucks' design already holds only Razorpay IDs, amounts and dates, with no bank or card numbers. Keep it that way. Add an itemised notice at sign-up for billing messages, separate consent for marketing and win-back, and a retention rule that keeps invoice records for the GST period while deleting other billing personal data on closure.

## The Razorpay account and the draft code each need work before go-live

The Razorpay account observed on 13 November 2026 is activated but "Limited" until KYC is complete. No website or app has been submitted for approval, which live keys require, and there are no plans, subscriptions or webhooks yet. That sets the critical path. Founding members who join now get three free months, and `billing_daily` queues the first link 10 days before a free period ends. So the first live link is due in early February 2027 for anyone onboarded in mid-November. Billing can be switched on in "status only" mode before live keys exist, but the Razorpay review, the Subscriptions and UPI Autopay enablement, and a written price quote (UPI subscription pricing is "available on request"; cards on subscriptions cost 0.9% plus the 2% platform fee ([Razorpay pricing](https://razorpay.com/pricing))) all need to be done by mid-January. At a 2% platform fee plus 18% GST on the fee, a ₹199 charge costs about ₹4.70, and a founding ₹99 charge about ₹2.35, before any subscription surcharge. Cashfree's flat model, at ₹7.5 per mandate and ₹5 per presentation under ₹1,000 ([Cashfree pricing](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/help/account/pricing)), is worse at these price points, which supports staying with Razorpay.

Reading `billing.sql`, `billing-link/index.ts` and `strings_billing.xml` alongside the research turns up six concrete defects. First, the founding discount is a Razorpay **50% offer**, which charges ₹99.50, ₹149.50 and ₹249.50, while `billing_plans.founding_price_paise` stores 9900, 14900 and 24900. The ledger, the copy and the debit will disagree by 50 paise. Second, `billing_link_queue` attaches the offer only `when s.founding and s.free_until > now()`. A founding member who approves during grace or after a pause gets no discount at all, which breaks a promise. Third, nothing warns a founding member before the price doubles at cycle 10. A silent step-up is the bait-and-switch pattern consumers already complain about. Fourth, `subscription.halted` and `subscription.paused` share one branch, which requests a new link. A customer who paused the mandate in their UPI app can only resume it there ([Razorpay UPI FAQs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/recurring-payments/upi/faqs?preferred-country=IN)), so a new link risks a second mandate and confusion. Fifth, there are two grace clocks. The daily job sets `paid_until + 7 days`, then `halted` resets the clock to `now() + 7 days`, so live time after a failed debit drifts between about 7 and 11 days. Sixth, reminders go out only as push notes, plus Razorpay's own SMS carrying the link from Razorpay's sender, and there are no WhatsApp or Bucks-branded messages. The things to keep are sound: server-side truth from signed webhooks, idempotent event storage, one free period per hashed phone and per plate, a profile kept intact while paused, a fresh mandate link after a halt, and the "billing off" switch.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the main risk from price to plumbing and proof. Bucks' prices are a fraction of what providers already pay elsewhere. The money will leak through registration drop-off, low-balance debits and silent revocations, and after the first quarter through providers who stopped receiving leads. That makes two things central to the module rather than nice extras: a lead counter tied to each billing cycle, and a rule that Bucks never charges a provider whom it failed to send leads to. They are what will keep monthly churn nearer IndiaMART's annual-plan 4% than its monthly-plan 7%.

The second insight concerns trust. In a market where 68% of people report subscription traps, the safest promise is also the best marketing: the exact amount, a mandate cap equal to the price, notice before every debit and before the founding step-up, and "stop anytime in your UPI app" said out loud. The regulations already guarantee most of this. Bucks gains by saying it plainly in the provider's own language and keeping its word on the founding price.

## Recommended module design

Priorities: **P0** is needed before the first live link (about mid-January 2027). **P1** is needed before the first renewals at scale (Q1 2027). **P2** improves the module once data arrives.

### States

The server keeps the existing statuses and adds two. A UI "retrying" sub-state is derived from the latest webhook.

| Status | Enter on | Live in search / vehicle online | Exit |
|---|---|---|---|
| FOUNDING / TRIAL (keep) | Listing goes live / vehicle approved | Yes | Free period ends → GRACE (no Autopay) or charge → ACTIVE |
| MANDATE_READY (new, P1, can be derived from `autopay=true` during a free period) | `subscription.authenticated` in a free period | Yes | First charge → ACTIVE |
| ACTIVE (keep) | `subscription.charged` | Yes | Due date passes unpaid → GRACE |
| GRACE with `retrying` flag (change, P0) | Free period ends unpaid, or `subscription.pending` | Yes | Charge → ACTIVE; grace end → PAUSED |
| USER_PAUSED (new, P0) | `subscription.paused` | No, after the paid period ends | `subscription.resumed` → ACTIVE. Do not send a new link while the mandate is paused |
| CANCELLED (keep) | `subscription.cancelled` | Until `paid_until` | → PAUSED |
| PAUSED (keep) | Grace over | No; profile, reviews and messages kept | Any charge or payment → ACTIVE |

Use **one grace clock** (P0): `grace_until = due_date + 7 days`, set once and never extended by `halted`. Seven days covers Razorpay's T+1 to T+3 retries plus four days for a manual payment. Keep it in `billing_config`.

### Pricing and offer changes

| Change | Priority |
|---|---|
| Replace the 50% offer with founding prices of exactly **₹99 / ₹149 / ₹249** (separate founding plans, or a flat-amount offer if Razorpay supports it; verify). Make `founding_price_paise` the single source | P0 |
| Apply the founding price whenever founding discount cycles remain, not only before `free_until`; count discounted cycles server-side | P0 |
| Notify before the step-up to full price at T-14 and T-3 days before cycle 10, with the new amount and date | P0 |
| Lock the founding price in writing ("₹99 until {month year}, then ₹199; we will tell you 14 days before") | P0 |
| Add a one-off **"pay this month"** Razorpay Payment Link (and annual prepay) for providers who refuse a mandate; handle `payment_link.paid` to extend `paid_until` | P1 |
| Show every price per day next to the monthly amount in all outside-app messages ("₹199/month, about ₹7 a day, 0% commission") | P1 |
| **Zero-lead guarantee**: if a paid cycle delivered fewer than N leads (calls plus WhatsApp taps), extend free for the next cycle automatically using the existing `billing_staff_extend` logic | P1 |
| Test a quarterly plan, and for Taxi a 7-day manual pass, before adding them to Autopay | P2 |
| Keep monthly as the default, yearly at 10× as an upgrade offered after 2–3 paid cycles | Keep |

### Channels and message schedule

Channel order: WhatsApp utility template first (for opted-in providers), then DLT-registered Bucks SMS (header with the -S/-T suffix), then push. The in-app plan card mirrors every state. Payment links travel only in WhatsApp and SMS, never in the app or in push. Send between 08:00 and 21:00 local time. Each message has one action and says "Bucks never asks for your UPI PIN or OTP". Razorpay's own receipts and failure emails stay on; Bucks adds context and does not duplicate them.

| When | Trigger | Bucks message (channel) | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Event | First lead during a free period | "Ramesh found you on Bucks. Keep customers coming for ₹7 a day. Lock your price now, first payment on {date}." Link (WhatsApp/SMS) | P1 |
| Trial day 23 (T-7) | 30-day trial | Lead summary, link, mandate explainer (WhatsApp/SMS); push says "check WhatsApp" | P0 (replaces the 10-day reminder) |
| Founding day ~60, then T-14 | 3-month founding period | Same, plus the founding price and when it steps up | P0 |
| T-3 | Free period ending, no mandate | Reminder, link re-sent (WhatsApp/SMS) | Keep (exists) |
| T-1 | Free period ending, no mandate | Push only | P1 |

### Metrics

Track each metric by plan, city, language and acquisition channel (field rep or self-serve). **Registration**: link sent → opened → authenticated, with time to authenticate (target to beat: Razorpay's ~30% drop). **Conversion**: free period → paid, separately for founding month 4 and month 13 and for trial day 30 (planning assumption 3–8%; anything above 10% is strong). **Debits**: first-attempt success, recovered by T+3, recovered in grace by manual link, and halted-to-paused. **Revocations and user pauses**, tracked separately from failures. **Monthly logo churn** by tenure (benchmark 5–7% on monthly plans). **Value**: leads per paying provider per cycle, share of payers with zero leads, and the zero-lead credits issued. **Cost**: Razorpay fee plus messaging cost per ₹100 collected. **Trust**: complaints, DLT complaint counts, and support contacts per 100 debits.
