# Safety, Moderation and Legal Risks: A Map of Trending / Most-Recommended Posts on Bucks (India, as of 9 Nov 2026)

Scope: a map layer showing posts and places (never live location of people) on an Indian local super-app whose posts get recommend / not-recommend votes. Research date: 9 November 2026. Many sources are law-firm commentary rather than gazette text. Verify the items marked **[VERIFY]** against MeitY / Gazette before relying on them.

## Q1. DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025: location as personal data, consent and notice, children, timelines, enforcement

### Takeaway
The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 Nov 2025 and come in three phases. Substantive duties (notice, consent, children's data, data principal rights) apply from **13 May 2027**. MeitY proposed in early 2026 to pull this forward to **13 Nov 2026**, but I found no confirmation that the change was notified, so it is still pending or uncertain. Once those duties apply, a post's precise geotag tied to an account is personal data. Bucks would then need itemised notice and consent for it. For under-18s it would need verifiable parental consent, and the Act flatly bans tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted ads aimed at them. That ban sits uneasily with personalised "trending near you" ranking for minors. I found no published Data Protection Board penalty order as of Nov 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Notification and phases**
- MeitY notified the DPDP Rules, 2025 on **13 Nov 2025** (G.S.R. 846(E) per one source, 843(E) per another). Phase I took effect immediately: Board set-up, definitions and procedure. Phase II, 12 months later (**13 Nov 2026**), covers consent-manager registration. Phase III, 18 months later (**13 May 2027**), covers notice and consent, fiduciary obligations, children's data, data principal rights, cross-border transfer, and the Board's substantive enforcement powers. — [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/?p=87811); [AMS Shardul alert](https://www.amsshardul.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Regulatory-Alert-Enforcement-of-DPDP-Act-and-Notification-of-DPDP-Rules.pdf)
- Sources conflict on when the children's verifiable-parental-consent rule starts. One Mondaq/AZB summary lists it as immediate. AMS Shardul and KS&K place it in the May 2027 tranche. CyberSigma says Nov 2026. The weight of law-firm sources says Rule 10 is in the 18-month tranche. — [AMS Shardul](https://www.amsshardul.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Regulatory-Alert-Enforcement-of-DPDP-Act-and-Notification-of-DPDP-Rules.pdf); [KS&K children's data](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/childrens-data-protection-under-indias-dpdp-rules/); [Mondaq (AZB)](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1713500/regulatory-update-notification-of-the-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025)
- **Pending:** MeitY asked industry, by **4 Feb 2026**, for views on cutting the data-fiduciary compliance window from 18 to 12 months, which would make the date **13 Nov 2026**. Reports disagree on whether this would apply to all fiduciaries or only Significant Data Fiduciaries. The proposal also floated bringing Rules 15 and 23 into force immediately and Rule 8(3) within three months. As of a 10 Mar 2026 source, industry feedback was still awaited. I found no notified amendment. — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/digital/meity-seeks-industry-views-on-fast-tracking-dpdp-act-rollout-proposes-12-month-compliance-timeline-88332.htm); [Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/india/privacy-protection/1760380/compression-of-dpdp-enforcement-timeline-proposed-by-meity); [dpdprules.org](https://dpdprules.org/blog/dpdp-18-months-to-12-months-proposal)

**Notice and consent**
- A notice must be clear and separate from other information. It must give an itemised description of the personal data, the specific purpose, a description of the goods, services or uses that the processing enables, and a link for withdrawing consent, exercising rights and complaining to the Board. — [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/?p=87811)
- Breaches go to the Board and affected users "without delay", followed by a detailed report within 72 hours. Separately, cyber incidents must be reported to CERT-In within 6 hours. — [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/?p=87811)
- Retention:
  - All fiduciaries must keep personal data, traffic data and logs for at least 1 year for specified government purposes.
  - Social media intermediaries with more than 20 million registered Indian users must erase data 3 years after a user's last engagement, with 48 hours' notice first.
  - Consent managers keep records for 7 years.
  — [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/?p=87811)
- Significant Data Fiduciaries must run an annual DPIA and audit and assess risks from algorithmic software. — [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/?p=87811)

**Is precise location "personal data"?**
- The Act's definition covers any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to that data. I took this from the general framing in the sources and did not verify it against the Act's text in this session **[VERIFY s.2(t)]**. Commentary on Section 3 confirms the Act's reach depends on identifiability.
- **Key nuance: Section 3(c)(ii) exclusion.** The Act does not apply to personal data that the data principal made, or caused to be made, publicly available. The Act's own illustration is a blogger posting her personal data on social media. Commentators disagree on scope. Some call it a blanket carve-out. Practitioners advise verifying and documenting the source rather than assuming everything online is exempt. One guide says Section 3 itself only comes into force on 13 May 2027. — [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-dpdp-act-publicly-available-personal-data-46899.html/); [law.asia](https://law.asia/publicly-available-data-dpdpa/); [JSA](https://www.jsalaw.com/articles-publications/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-striking-a-balance-between-privacy-rights-and-public-data/); [Myndsolution DPDP wiki](https://dpdp.myndsolution.com/wiki/guides/who-does-the-act-apply-to/)

**Children (under 18)**
- Section 9(3) bars tracking or behavioural monitoring of children and targeted advertising directed at them. Commentators say parental consent cannot make these lawful. — [Tsaaro](https://tsaaro.com/blogs/safeguarding-minors-online-understanding-parental-consent-obligations-and-behavioural-monitoring-restrictions-under-the-dpdpa-and-dpdp-rules); [CyberPeace](https://cyberpeace.org/resources/blogs/prohibition-of-behavioral-tracking-and-targeted-advertising-for-children-under-the-dpdp-act-2023)
- Rule 10 requires verifiable parental consent and due diligence that the parent is an identifiable adult (18+). This can use details the fiduciary already holds, or a virtual token from an authorised entity. One source mentions DigiLocker-based tokens. — [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/?p=87811); [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/childrens-data-protection-under-indias-dpdp-rules/)
- The Fourth Schedule (with Rule 12) exempts healthcare, educational institutions, crèches/childcare and child transport from verifiable consent and the monitoring ban, and only for welfare, safety or education purposes. Social and consumer apps are not exempt. — [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/?p=87811); [Tsaaro](https://tsaaro.com/blogs/safeguarding-minors-online-understanding-parental-consent-obligations-and-behavioural-monitoring-restrictions-under-the-dpdpa-and-dpdp-rules)

**Enforcement**
- The Board was constituted with effect from 13 Nov 2025. Reports differ on whether it is operational. An Apr 2026 ET report (via pulse.bot) said selection panels had not yet been notified. A Mar 2026 guide said the Board had not started operations. — [pulse.bot / ET](https://www.pulse.bot/legal/news/central-government-yet-to-notify-selection-panels-for-data-protection-board-a0d14e70-97d4-42ca-9318-ef16a8c320bf/); [consentos](https://consentos.pages.dev/learn/dpdp-penalties-and-enforcement); [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/11/decrypting-india-s-data-protection-regime-the-data)
- The maximum penalty is ₹250 crore per instance, for failing to keep reasonable security safeguards. Appeals go to the Appellate Tribunal (TDSAT) within 60 days. — [TCSA](https://www.tcsa.in/frameworks/dpdp/penalties-enforcement); [Unified Chambers](https://www.unifiedchambers.com/blog/first-dpb-penalty-orders-analysis)

### Inferences
- **Precise geotags are personal data.** A precise geotag on a post, linked to a handle, identifies a person's whereabouts at a point in time. Repeated geotags reveal home, school or work patterns, so the data is very likely personal data. Bucks should not rely on the 3(c)(ii) "publicly available" carve-out for the map:
  - The user published the post, but Bucks derives and aggregates the location.
  - Commentators expect source verification.
  - Section 3 may not even be in force until May 2027.
- **Notice and consent.** The map is a new purpose: placing a post on a public map and ranking by place. Before May 2027 (or Nov 2026 if the compression is notified), Bucks's notice should list location as a separate item and get separate, withdrawable consent for showing the post on the map.
- **Minors and personalised map ranking.** Ranking "trending near you" for a minor from their device location plausibly counts as "tracking / behavioural monitoring", which no consent can cure. The safest designs are:
  - Minors' posts are excluded from the map.
  - Minors' map view is non-personalised: city or area level, with no history-based ranking.
- **Plan to the earlier date.** Build to 13 Nov 2026 as if the compression applies. If it does not, May 2027 is the hard deadline.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the official Gazette text of the Act or Rules in this session. The exact Rule numbers and the s.2(t) wording need checking against MeitY's text.
- Whether the 18-to-12-month compression was notified is unknown (no source after Mar 2026 found).
- No Data Protection Board orders found. Whether the Board is operational as of Nov 2026 is unclear.
- Whether MeitY has notified any class of fiduciary that may process children's data above a set age (s.9(5)) was not found.

## Q2. IT Rules 2021 (intermediary due diligence) and the 2025–2026 amendments

### Takeaway
As a host of user-generated content (UGC), Bucks is an intermediary and keeps safe harbour (s.79 IT Act) only if it does the IT Rules due diligence: a grievance officer and published rules, and fast takedowns. The **Feb 2026 amendment (notified 10 Feb 2026, in force 20 Feb 2026)** sharply shortened deadlines:
- 3 hours for government and court takedown orders, down from 36 hours.
- Faster handling of non-consensual intimate imagery (NCII) and grievances (figures differ by source).
- Labelling and provenance rules for AI / synthetically generated information (SGI).

Bucks becomes a Significant Social Media Intermediary (SSMI) at 5 million registered Indian users and then takes on extra duties.

### Cited Findings
- **Oct 2025 amendment.** MeitY issued it on 22 Oct 2025, in force 15 Nov 2025. It restructured the Rule 3(1)(d) takedown procedure but kept the 36-hour window. It does not require informing affected users or publishing takedown directions. — [Argus Partners](https://www.argus-p.com/updates/updates/revisiting-indias-digital-intermediary-framework-insights-from-the-2025-amendment/); [Sabrang India](https://sabrangindia.in/synthetic-content-three-hour-compliance-and-the-risk-of-over-removal-analysing-the-it-rules-amendments/)
- **Feb 2026 amendment.** Notified 10 Feb 2026, effective 20 Feb 2026.
  - SGI means audio, visual or audio-visual content "artificially or algorithmically created, generated, modified, or altered" that appears real.
  - Permitted SGI must be prominently labelled and carry permanent metadata or provenance markers, and platforms must not allow those markers to be removed.
  - Government or court orders must be actioned within 3 hours, down from 36.
  - SSMIs (more than 5 million registered Indian users) must collect user declarations on SGI, verify them, and label confirmed SGI. An SSMI that knowingly allows non-compliant SGI is "deemed to have failed its due diligence obligations".
  — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/india-introduces-mandatory-labelling-for-ai-and-3hour-takedown-for-illegal-content); [AMS Shardul](https://www.amsshardul.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Legislative-Update-IT-Rules-Amendment.pdf)
- **Conflicting figures on the other timelines:**
  - Bar & Bench, TICE and ThePrint report NCII cut from 24 hours to **2 hours** and complaint resolution from 72 hours to **36 hours**.
  - KS&K's table instead shows grievance acknowledgment cut from 24 hours to 2 hours, general court orders from 15 days to 7 days, and specified-content orders (rape or CSAM depictions, re-uploads) from 72 hours to 36 hours.
  - One source says the grievance deadline was halved from 15 to 7 days.
  - **[VERIFY against rule text]**
  — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/amp/story/news/law-policy/centre-amends-it-rules-to-regulate-ai-generated-content-takedown-timelines-reduced); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/governance/36-hr-takedown-window-cut-to-3-hrs-centre-notifies-it-rules-amendment-to-tighten-noose-on-deepfakes/2851104); [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/information-technology/indias-2026-it-rules-on-synthetic-media-explained/)
- **Other duties under the Feb 2026 amendment:**
  - Users must be told of the rules at least once every 3 months.
  - Intermediaries offering SGI-creation tools must block CSAM, NCII, false documents, arms and explosives content, and deceptive impersonation.
  - Violators must have accounts suspended without destroying evidence. A victim complainant can be given the violator's identity. Specified offences must be reported.
  — [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/information-technology/indias-2026-it-rules-on-synthetic-media-explained/)
- Commentators warn that 3-hour windows encourage over-removal of lawful content. — [Sabrang India](https://sabrangindia.in/synthetic-content-three-hour-compliance-and-the-risk-of-over-removal-analysing-the-it-rules-amendments/)

### Inferences
- A map layer adds its own takedown routes: pin or post removal, and suppression of a place's tile or heat. Bucks needs on-call moderation able to act within 3 hours around the clock on government and court orders. It also needs a fast lane for NCII and doxxing (home address, school) reports, targeting 2 hours to match the strictest reading.
- If Bucks offers AI filters or generators, SGI labelling must carry through into map previews and thumbnails.
- Map pins of "trending" posts may draw police or government blocking orders during communal tension or protests. Bucks needs a geofenced "kill switch" to disable the map layer per district.

### Gaps
- No primary text of the Feb 2026 notification was read. The NCII and grievance timelines conflict across sources.
- Whether the grievance officer must be resident in India (true for SSMIs under the 2021 Rules) was not re-confirmed for 2026.

## Q3. Documented harms from location features

### Takeaway
There is a well-documented pattern. Opt-in location maps (Snap Map 2017, Instagram Map Aug 2025) triggered child-safety warnings from police, NSPCC, US senators and 37 state attorneys general. Aggregated heat maps (Strava 2018) exposed sensitive sites even when "anonymised". A 2026 ITV investigation reports offenders using Snap Map to find hotspots where minors gather. I found no verified Indian case of stalking via EXIF or geotags specifically, but stalking through Instagram is routinely prosecuted under BNS s.78.

### Cited Findings
- **Snap Map, 2017.** NSPCC, schools and UK police warned at launch. NSPCC said even "friends only" sharing is risky if contacts include strangers, and cited Netaware data that 36% of young people had added a stranger in the past 6 months. Snap said location sharing is opt-in and off by default. — [Grimsby Telegraph](https://www.grimsbytelegraph.co.uk/news/snapchat-snap-map-nspcc-warning-180757); [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2017/07/06/schools-warn-snapchat-map-location-sharing-puts-children-risk); [Silicon UK](https://www.silicon.co.uk/mobility/police-warn-privacy-dangers-snapchat-maps-215839/amp)
- **ITV / STV, 2026.** Offenders were reported "repeatedly using Snapchat to locate children in real life", with the location function helping them find hotspots where minors interact. Dozens were sentenced in 2026. Ofcom announced in May (2026) that Snapchat had committed to changes stopping adults contacting children. The NSPCC wants under-18 "Safe Accounts" with location off by default. — [STV News](https://news.stv.tv/world/a-safe-haven-for-paedophiles-thousands-of-children-abused-on-snapchat)
- **Instagram Map.** Launched 6 Aug 2025. Users said they were not told before their location became visible, and geotagged stories appeared on the map even for people who had opted out of live sharing.
  - Senators Blackburn and Blumenthal asked Meta to "immediately abandon" the feature.
  - 37 state attorneys general asked Meta to disable it for minors.
  - Meta said it is off by default, parents get a notification when a teen turns it on, and it would make the map clearer.
  — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/bipartisan-senators-urge-meta-roll-back-instagram-maps-feature-rcna223938); [Utah News Dispatch](https://utahnewsdispatch.com/?p=12245); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/instagrams-new-map-feature-faces-backlash-over-privacy-concerns-despite-metas-reassurances-488794-2025-08-11)
- **Strava heatmap, 2018.** The aggregated heatmap outlined military bases and patrol routes, with sparse forward bases in Syria and Afghanistan standing out. Private activities and privacy zones were already excluded. Researcher Lukasz Olejnik warned that anonymising location data is hard even when aggregated. — [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2018/01/29/strava-user-heatmap-reveals-patterns-of-life-in-western-military-bases/); [BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/stravas-heatmap-reveals-military-bases-and-more)
- **Snap disables heatmap.** Snap temporarily switched off the public Snap Map heatmap in Ukraine in March 2022 for public safety. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/snapchat-disables-heatmap-feature-ukraine-154342214.html?src=rss)
- **EXIF.** The FBI identified a hacker from GPS EXIF data in a photo he posted. Instagram strips most metadata on upload, but a manual location tag can still appear. — [CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/535700/embedded-data-not-breasts-brought-down-hacker.html); [Exposing the Invisible](https://www.exposingtheinvisible.org/en/articles/more-than-meets-the-eye-investigating)
- **Indian cases.** Instagram-linked stalking is prosecuted under BNS s.78 (stalking) and IT Act s.67. Examples include a Bengaluru voyeur posting photos of women on Instagram and a Thane morphing case. None mentions geotags or EXIF as the method. — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india/karnataka/bengaluru/bengaluru-man-arrested-for-taking-voyeuristic-photos-of-women-posting-them-on-instagram-3623692); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/thane-instagram-stalker-uses-photos-of-young-woman-to-create-obscene-videos-ambernath-police-register-case); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/foreign-woman-tourist-films-2-men-following-her-for-photos-in-mumbai-duo-booked-for-stalking/2865921)
- **UK ICO Children's Code, standard 10.** Geolocation should be off by default for children. An ICO review found platforms typically do this, though some nudge children to turn it on. — [ICO progress update, Aug 2024](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/children-s-code-strategy-progress-update/)
- **Ofcom Protection of Children Codes.** Published 24 Apr 2025, in effect from 25 Jul 2025, including recommender-system measures. At consultation stage, a default-settings measure on user location applied only to services at high risk of grooming. — [RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/technology-digital/summer-2025/ofcom-publishes-childrens-online-safety-codes-and-risk-assessment-guidance/); [OSA Network annex](https://ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/consultations/category-1-10-weeks/284469-consultation-protecting-children-from-harms-online/responses/online-safety-act-network-annex-a---measures-table---childrens-update.pdf)

### Inferences
- Even without live location, a "posts on a map" layer leaks in three ways:
  - Precise pins on posts from homes, hostels, PGs or schools.
  - Repeated posts by one user that cluster around the same place (pattern-of-life).
  - "Trending" hotspots that show where young people gather.
- Women and minors in Indian cities are the highest-risk groups, given the volume of BNS s.78 stalking cases.
- The Instagram Map backlash shows that confusing UI ("am I on the map?") causes as much reputational damage as actual leaks.

### Gaps
- No NCMEC data specific to location features was found.
- No final Ofcom code text on location defaults was read.
- No verified Indian EXIF or geotag stalking case was found. The researcher did not locate one; this is a gap, not evidence of absence.

## Q4. Abuse of local rankings: fake reviews, vote rings, disguised promotion

### Takeaway
India's CCPA Dark Patterns Guidelines (in force **30 Nov 2023**) treat fake reviews and false ratings as "disguised advertisement", which is a prohibited dark pattern. BIS **IS 19000:2022** (Nov 2022) sets standards for online reviews: disclose paid reviews and ban purchased ones. Compliance with it is still described as voluntary, and I found no notification making it mandatory. A recommend / not-recommend vote that ranks businesses or places on a map functions as a rating, so a manipulated or paid "most recommended" pin carries Consumer Protection Act exposure.

### Cited Findings
- The CCPA Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023 took effect 30 Nov 2023 and list 13 specified dark patterns. Fake user reviews and false ratings fall under "disguised advertisement". — [Ahlawat & Associates](https://www.ahlawatassociates.com/news/guidelines-for-prevention-and-regulations-of-dark-patterns); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/ccpas-guidelines-on-dark-patterns-an-overview/1844140)
- A June 2025 CCPA advisory required e-commerce platforms to self-audit for dark patterns within 3 months. — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!bharat/ccpa-issues-advisory-to-e-commerce-platforms-for-self-audit-to-detect-dark-patterns-enn25060704136); [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/dark-patterns-and-the-limits-of-self-policing-a-critical-look-at-the-ccpa-s-june-2025-advisory)
- Consumers can report suspected dark patterns, including fake reviews, through the Jagriti app, which counts as a formal CCPA complaint. — [CAG](https://www.cag.org.in/blogs/regulating-trust-indias-legal-response-fake-online-reviews)
- **IS 19000:2022.** Issued Nov 2022 and applies to any organisation publishing consumer reviews online.
  - Reviewers must be identifiable.
  - Paid or sponsored reviews must be disclosed.
  - Purchased reviews, or reviews by people hired to write them, are barred.
  - Editing reviews to change their message and discouraging negative reviews are barred.
  - The government said compliance would start voluntary and become mandatory "if the menace continues to grow". A 2026 article says the government has signalled intent to make it mandatory, with no notified date.
  — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/1038007/scroll_in); [CAG](https://www.cag.org.in/blogs/regulating-trust-indias-legal-response-fake-online-reviews); [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/google-review-policy-changes-2026-and-india-s-legal-framework-against-fake-online-reviews)
- CCPA enforcement in general: 325 notices on misleading ads and ₹1.19 crore in penalties (date per article). I found no confirmed 2025–26 CCPA penalty specifically for fake reviews. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/advertising/ccpa-issues-325-notices-for-violation-of-consumer-rights-misleading-ads-imposes-penalties-of-rs-1-19-crore-50910.htm)

### Inferences
- Paid boosts of a business pin must be labelled "Sponsored" and kept visually separate from organic "most recommended" pins. Otherwise Bucks risks the disguised-advertisement and false-urgency dark patterns.
- Vote rings (merchants buying recommends, rivals buying not-recommends) are the core integrity risk for a place-ranked map. Following IS 19000 voluntarily (identifiable voters, disclosed incentives, no suppression of negatives) is cheap and helps defend Bucks.
- Showing "trending" or "X people nearby recommend" on thin data risks false social proof. Show counts only above a minimum threshold.

### Gaps
- Whether IS 19000 is mandatory as of Nov 2026: not confirmed (treat as voluntary but likely).
- No CCPA order specifically on fake reviews was found.
- Whether a social app (as opposed to an e-commerce entity) falls within the Dark Patterns Guidelines depends on whether Bucks is a "platform" offering goods or services. The super-app's commerce features make that more likely. This is inference, not sourced.

## Q5. Industry mitigations and the resulting risk register

### Takeaway
Industry practice and regulator guidance point to the same safeguards:
- Location off by default, and off entirely for minors.
- Coarse or venue-snapped locations instead of precise pins.
- Time delay and expiry (Snap shows public snaps for about 24 hours with usernames stripped).
- ML filtering of what reaches the map.
- Suppression of sparse cells (the lesson from Strava).
- The ability to switch the map off per region (Snap in Ukraine).
- Clear "you are on the map" UI (the lesson from Instagram).

### Cited Findings
- **Snap Map "Our Story".** Heat shows where public submissions come from. Snaps appear for about 24 hours with usernames stripped. Machine learning surfaces only Snaps judged safe, so not every submission appears. Friend location is opt-in, with Ghost Mode, and parents can see whom a teen shares location with. — [ithinkdiff](https://www.ithinkdiff.com/snap-map-view-global-stories-on-a-map-from-friends-and-places-on-snapchat/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/snapchat-adds-new-safety-features-including-live-location-requests/732983/); [Mic](https://mic.com/articles/180540/snapchat-map-new-snap-map-update-highlights-the-key-difference-between-snap-and-instagram)
- **Instagram Map.** Off by default. Tagged content shows for a limited 24 hours. Parental supervision notifies parents and lets them disable it for teens. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/instagram-maps-feature-raises-privacy-concerns-some-users-rcna223706)
- **Strava.** Private activities and privacy zones were excluded, and users can opt out of the heatmap. Even so, sparse regions leaked sensitive sites. — [BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/stravas-heatmap-reveals-military-bases-and-more)
- **Snap regional switch-off.** Snap disabled the heatmap in Ukraine in March 2022. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/snapchat-disables-heatmap-feature-ukraine-154342214.html?src=rss)
- **ICO standard 10.** Children's geolocation off by default. — [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/children-s-code-strategy-progress-update/)
